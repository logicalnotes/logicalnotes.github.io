Sisyphus, a man doomed to failure, yet who keeps hauling his boulder up to the mountain summit time and again. With futile persistence, he proclaims to fate: even with no hope in sight, he will not yield.

He was once a clever, scheming mortal king in ancient Greek mythology. For mocking the gods and defying their rules, Zeus condemned him to an eternal punishment: to push a huge boulder from the foot of the mountain to its peak. Yet every time he neared the top, the boulder would roll back down. The ordeal repeats forever, without end.

It is futile, utterly meaningless. Sisyphus ought to be tormented. But Albert Camus, the existentialist philosopher, argued that Sisyphus’ predicament mirrors our absurd human condition. We wake up each day to go to school or work. Assessments and exams bring constant anxiety amid endless rat‑racing. We barely get through one phase of life, only to be confronted with harder challenges ahead. You finish an exam and finally breathe easy, just to hear your teacher say the next second: “Mid‑terms are coming up, everyone keep pushing.”

That sounds exactly like Sisyphus pushing his boulder. Camus closed his work with a striking line: *One must imagine Sisyphus happy*.

What does this mean? Here is one way to understand it: even if the world appears meaningless and cyclical, we get to choose how we respond to life. Knowing full well the boulder will tumble down, we still choose to heave it upward. This stubborn resolve to carry on, even when hope is nowhere to be found, represents the freest and most unyielding way to live.

Some accuse Camus of romanticizing suffering. To me, however, he offers us a way to face reality bravely. We may know certain endeavours hold no inherent meaning, yet we choose to pursue them anyway — and forge our own stance in the process. Much like persisting, even when you know hard work may not guarantee rewards.

Sisyphus is a mythological figure, yet far more than that. He could be you. He could be me. He could be every person clinging on through life, refusing to give in even amid hardship.
