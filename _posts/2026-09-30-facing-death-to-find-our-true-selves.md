If you only had three days left to live, what would you do?
Would you head home to spend those final days with your parents? Would you finally confess your feelings to someone you like? Or would you travel, go skydiving, meet new people, and soak in everything the world has to offer? What would you choose?

Everyone would answer differently. Yet one thing holds true: when we honestly face the fact that we will die, our minds become crystal‑clear. We suddenly know what we want, what matters to us, and what we need to let go of. We get in touch with our truest selves. That lies at the heart of what it means to be‑toward‑death.

Coined by German philosopher Martin Heidegger in his major work *Being and Time*, “being‑toward‑death” can be put simply: when we fully accept that death is every human’s unavoidable final destination, we break free from mindless routine. We can then focus on what is authentic and meaningful, and live as our genuine selves.

Heidegger argued that most people do not live by their own true will. We follow other people’s expectations and societal norms, unsure of who we really want to be. Heidegger calls this state “fallenness”.

The way out of this state is to actively and bravely confront death in our minds and outlook. Only when we keep in mind that our lives will one day end can we move toward authentic existence. We stop sleepwalking through life following other people’s scripts. This realization brings true freedom — we can then make our own life choices with clarity and resolve.

For example, many people grow anxious over what others think of them. Yet if you consider that decades from now everyone will pass away, those opinions will mean nothing. You realise there is no point wasting energy on other people’s judgements.

Some people fear failure. They hold back from pursuing hobbies or the people they care about. But remembering that we all die in the end, what is there to lose from failing? This thought gives them the courage to take that first step. These are all real‑life examples of being‑toward‑death at work.

In daily life, most of us feel young. We are in our teens or twenties, and death feels far away. We avoid thinking about it. We tell ourselves there is plenty of time. We keep saying “later”, “sometime”, “when I get the chance”. But there may not be many “laters”, and chances are never guaranteed. We never know when death may come.

Being‑toward‑death means seeing life’s finish line plainly before you, and waking up to how you ought to live. It helps you sort out your most vital bonds and connections. It works like a thought experiment: place your end point right in front of you, and rethink how you live today.

The things you would do if you had only three days left? You can start doing them right now. The person who first crosses your mind? Give them a call or send them a message today.

Life is a finite gift. Death’s shadow is not here to devour us. It exists so that the light of our lives can shine brighter and with sharper focus. You do not need to count down every single day. But you can make your days count.

Living by the idea of being‑toward‑death is like calibrating a compass every so often. It realigns your life direction and helps you rediscover your authentic self, buried beneath everyday chaos.
