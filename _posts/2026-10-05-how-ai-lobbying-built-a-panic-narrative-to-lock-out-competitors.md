A resignation post hit 160 million views. Let’s unpack the chilling closed-loop scheme of AI and its funding backers. Who’s pulling the strings? A regular tech worker issued a doomsday warning. How did it blow up across Washington in just hours?

A young technician, only three months into his new job, suddenly posted his resignation letter online. He claimed AI could wipe out humanity within a decade. Within 24 hours, this post racked up 130 million reads.

Then something surreal unfolded. Seven senators, two governors and thirteen congressmen lined up to post on social media almost overnight, all repeating the same message: cutting-edge AI must be reined in immediately, and the federal government has to take control.

His name is Jacob Cox, 27. Three months prior, he joined Anthropic, the company behind Claude. Before that, he was virtually unknown in the industry, with a dormant social media account.

Why did the warning from an ordinary tech employee trigger such a massive reaction in Washington within hours? Was he a lone whistleblower speaking out of conscience?

Let’s map out the timeline minute by minute. Eighteen minutes before he hit send, an exclusive in-depth interview from The Wall Street Journal had already gone live across the web. The article quoted his resignation letter verbatim, complete with his alarming claims that top labs were spiraling out of control.

That’s not all. Two minutes before his post went live, the lead of an AI think tank called AI Policy Network had already shared the interview.

Six minutes after the post was published, general counsel from lobbying group Encode AI backed his claims. At the ninth minute, the head of AI Futures Project amplified the story. Within half an hour, program directors from AI safety funding bodies joined in precisely on cue.

Within fifteen minutes, a coordinated group of funders, lobbyists and safety experts appeared in unison. It’s impossible that everyone just happened to stumble on the post at the exact same time. This was no spontaneous event. It was a pre-planned, coordinated media push.

What’s the connection between these people? Simply check the public tax filings of these nonprofits. The funding trail is crystal clear.

The AI Futures Project received $2.5 million in grants. $500k went to lobbying firm Encode AI, and $2 million went to AI Policy Network, who shared the article two minutes early. All the money came from one single source: the Survival and Flourishing Fund.

Who is the major donor and core advisor of this foundation? Dustin Moskovitz, co-founder of Facebook. He also holds a far more critical role: an early key investor and board observer at Anthropic.

What about the man who wrote the resignation letter? Back in 2022, when he was still in his early twenties, he won a $20,000 Long Term Future Fellowship funded by Dustin Moskovitz, Facebook co-founder and one of Anthropic’s biggest early investors.

Now the picture gets suspicious. This same group of early investors built a massive ecosystem with their capital. They gave scholarships to the whistleblower, seed money to think tanks spreading the story, and operating budgets to lobbying groups providing public endorsements.

But it doesn’t stop there. This funding even reached newsrooms at established mainstream outlets.

They launched the Tarbell Reporter Fellowship. It directly funds journalists at major publications to cover stories centered on AI’s supposed existential risk. The Guardian has received over $3 million total from this funding network.

A perfect panic loop was created. Reporters writing these stories receive their grants. The so-called independent experts quoted get research stipends from them. The alarming studies cited in articles are funded by their grants.

Many long investigative pieces loudly claim to be objective and independent at the bottom, while hiding who actually funds them.

Even many popular tech creators with millions of followers have publicly admitted they were offered large grants. The contract had one strict requirement: their videos must heavily emphasize AI’s existential threat to humanity.

They spent tens of millions of dollars stoking public fear. What’s in it for them? It’s not just burning cash for fun. In Washington, no one pays purely for public emotion. Every carefully engineered panic eventually heads to congressional hearings, to be turned into binding legal privileges.

Five days before this resignation story exploded online, Senator Sanders and Representative Castle introduced landmark legislation in Congress: the Superintelligence Ban Act. The bill was extremely harsh.

First: halt all research and development of advanced models.
Second: create a new cabinet-level federal regulator with full lifecycle mandatory oversight over all frontier large language models.
Third: violators could face up to 20 years in federal prison, and companies could be dissolved — a corporate death penalty.

Here’s the key question. Who runs this all-powerful cabinet agency? Who sets the rules? The bill states clearly that an AI expert advisory committee will guide its work.

Ask yourself who will end up sitting on that committee. The safety scholars, think tank leaders and lobbying advisors funded for years by this financial network.

At this point, every gear of the machine locks into place. In political economy terms, this is a textbook case of regulatory capture.

Big tech investors fund think tanks, academics, media and online creators. They frame technical risks as an imminent global apocalypse, stoke public anxiety, and push politicians to expand regulatory power.

Then they push through laws, erecting high-barrier, cumbersome federal licensing review systems. The power to write rules and issue permits goes straight to their allies.

What’s the end result of this strategy?

Closed-source giants with billions for compliance and seasoned Washington lobbyists can afford legal teams and clear all checks to obtain licenses. Once licensed, they lock the gate from the inside.

Independent open-source developers, garage startups and small university teams cannot afford millions for compliance paperwork. Even modifying a single line of code leaves them facing 20 years behind bars or corporate dissolution.

Wrapping themselves in the noble banner of saving humanity, they build a moat protected by federal criminal law to block all competitors.

The cage forged in the name of safety will never contain real technological risks. Its only purpose is to shut the door on new entrants.
