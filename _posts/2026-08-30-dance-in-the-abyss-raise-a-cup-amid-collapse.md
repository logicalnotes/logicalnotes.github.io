Have you ever stayed up late? Why do some people cannot help scrolling on their phones deep into the night? Beneath this seemingly modern‑day question lies an echo of an ancient spirit.

The Dionysian spirit is a crucial aesthetic and life‑affirming concept put forward by Friedrich Nietzsche, originating from Dionysus, the Greek god of wine. To Nietzsche, the Dionysian spirit embodies humanity’s most primal life forces: desire, passion, chaos, breakdown, and irrationality — all wellsprings of artistic creation. Its counterpart is the Apollonian spirit, which pursues reason, order and logic.

Nietzsche believed that when human society chases order and reason excessively, it stifles vital human instincts. This drains cultural vitality and gives rise to nihilism.

In today’s fast‑paced world, we are constantly told to plan our work, keep our emotions in check, and conduct ourselves appropriately. Constrained by countless social rules, people inevitably crave moments of release. Many remain rational and restrained by day, only to surrender to uninhibited indulgence after dark. Young people reconnect with the Dionysian impulse in diverse ways: late‑night clubbing, drinking to unwind, mindless late‑night scrolling, impulsive spending. These acts that look unproductive, reckless and irrational are instinctive push‑backs against rigid order and reality — echoes of the Dionysian spirit in the 21st century.

Still, the Dionysian spirit is not mere self‑indulgence or decadence. Nietzsche intended to remind us: life cannot consist solely of plans and rationality; it also calls for chaos and passion. Those who embrace the Dionysian spirit accept that we cannot control everything in life. Yet they dare to dance through suffering, and strike a balance between reason and passion amid humdrum routines.

To live a rigid, predictable, rule‑bound existence is not life’s true meaning. True life is fervent, turbulent, painful, yet creative. It is like a drunken dance, where chaos mingles with order. This is the Dionysian spirit.
