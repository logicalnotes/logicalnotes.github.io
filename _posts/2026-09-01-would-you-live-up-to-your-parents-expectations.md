Do you follow your parents’ advice? Or would you live up to your parents’ expectations?

When you ponder this question, it is far more than simply deciding whether you should obey your parents. It raises a profound philosophical question: Who am I? Am I the ideal answer sheet written by my parents, or the authentic narrative of my own life?

This reveals the conflict between personal freedom and others’ expectations, as well as the tension between independence and compliance.

Kant stated: “Enlightenment is man’s emergence from his self‑incurred immaturity.” When you merely mechanically fulfil other people’s demands, you have not truly grown up.

Sartre put it more bluntly: “Man is condemned to be free.” This means you are destined to make your own choices. You cannot hand your fate over to others. Sartre encouraged us to follow our inner voice, even if it may trigger conflicts. He believed human suffering stems from failing to follow one’s own inner choices.

This reminds us: do not merely play the roles others assign you — the obedient child, the good student, the qualified adult. Instead, pause and reflect on what kind of life you truly want.

The hardest part of life is not saying no, but figuring out what you genuinely desire.

Let us circle back to our original question: Would you live up to your parents’ expectations?

The very act of reflecting on this question already sets you on the path toward freedom. You are re‑examining the relationship between others’ expectations and your true self.

Freedom is not running away from your parents’ control. It is realizing that you always hold a choice. You may choose compliance, or you may choose resistance. What matters is whether the path aligns with who you truly want to be. You can certainly live up to your parents’ expectations — but only if that is also what you want for yourself.
