Do you ever dwell on things you wish you hadn’t done?
A time you stumbled over your words mid-conversation,
An exam result that fell short of what you hoped for,
An awkward social moment where you froze and couldn’t think of the right reply.

These small regrets should fade quickly, yet for some people, they loop over and over in their heads. They turn into an endless cycle of self-judgment and self-bullying — often without the person even realising it. What exactly is self-bullying?

Broadly speaking, it means repeatedly treating yourself harshly or attacking your own mind. It takes the form of harsh self-criticism, self-shame, and constant putting-down. Common signs include running lines like “I’m useless” or “I don’t deserve good things” through your mind, and calling yourself cruel names: weak, worthless, a failure. It often goes hand in hand with perfectionism — setting impossibly high standards, punishing yourself hard for any misstep, and unable to let go of mistakes or rejections. In more serious cases, it fuels social anxiety and makes you pull away from the world.

Why does this happen?

For many, it starts in childhood. Constant criticism, put-downs or neglect from parents, caregivers or other important people get internalised. You come to believe deep down: I am not good enough. I am unlovable. The voice that hurt you outside becomes a voice inside your own head.

If you are naturally a perfectionist and struggle to manage your emotions, setbacks and stress make it far easier to turn that anger inward. And environments full of pressure and comparison only make it worse.

Self-bullying leaves lasting, far-reaching harm. It erodes self-respect, raises the risk of anxiety and depression, drains motivation and your sense of worth. It holds you back at school and work, and can even take a toll on your physical health.

So how do you break this pattern?

Try to look at your flaws as an outside observer would. Don’t blow small mistakes out of proportion, don’t stick hurtful labels on yourself, and accept that imperfection is human. Gradually swap that harsh inner voice with kinder self-talk, and build small wins through steady action.

Stop torturing yourself over old regrets. Remember: the fact you feel regret means you understand more now than you did back then. That is growth, and it’s something to acknowledge.

Life has far more room for error than we imagine. One misstep, one awkward moment, does not ruin everything. It’s okay if you didn’t get into your dream school, land the perfect job, or close a chapter of love without hurt.

Life is not an exam where you need full marks. It is a long road where falling down, taking detours, and correcting course are all allowed. We grow by trial and error, and it is these rough, imperfect parts that make us who we are.

When you give life room to be messy, you untie the weight you carry. Learn to leave space for yourself, cut yourself some slack. You’ll find that being gentle with yourself is strength, too.
