Sigmund Freud once said, “Unexpressed emotions will never die. They are buried alive and will come forth later in uglier ways.”

I could not agree more.

Nowadays, “emotional stability” is widely celebrated online as the ultimate mark of maturity. Yet many of us misunderstand what it truly means. We mistake suppression for self‑restraint, and numbness for inner peace. True emotional stability never asks us to erase joy, anger, grief or delight. It should never be achieved by forcing ourselves to bottle up feelings. When we push grievances, rage and sorrow deep down, we may appear calm on the surface. But those unseen, unprocessed feelings do not vanish. They pile up and fester inside us, gradually giving rise to anxiety, endless mental exhaustion, self‑doubt and other psychological struggles.

I once held this harsh expectation for myself: to remain unshaken, whether met with others’ furious outbursts or lavish praise. Later I realized only the dead can be entirely free of emotional ripples. We are human beings of flesh and blood, born with a full range of feelings. No matter how much we work on inner growth, we may still burn with anger when slandered by others. We may still sink into overwhelming grief when we lose a beloved family member. These reactions do not prove we are immature; they are simply what it means to be alive.

Emotions themselves are neither good nor bad. Anger signals that our boundaries have been violated. Grief is nature’s way of healing loss. Fear serves as our in‑built survival instinct. To repress emotions is to ignore signals sent from within. Only when we let our feelings flow and release them appropriately, without resistance or avoidance, can we live with vitality.

Read works of psychology and philosophy, and you will encounter ideas that seem contradictory at first glance. If we move past black‑and‑white thinking and turn inward for reflection, their underlying logic reveals itself. To move past grief, we must first allow ourselves to fully experience sorrow and longing. To transcend weakness, we must dare to face our fragile, vulnerable sides.

Transformation happens only through acceptance. Admit your fragility instead of pretending to be invincible, and you will gain genuine strength. Acknowledge your fears instead of forcing yourself to be fearless, and you will slowly build courage to face hardship. Recognize your limits instead of demanding perfection from yourself, and you will unlock greater possibilities for growth.

Emotional stability does not mean feeling nothing when storms hit. It means you do not force yourself to calm down instantly when waves of feeling rise. Do not bury negative emotions deep inside. Stop judging yourself for feeling angry or sad. See every emotion for what it is, accept your whole self, and give your inner experiences space to exist. When we stop burying our true selves, we finally reach a place of genuine composure and inner stability.
